Research

Research

Machine-checkable temporal semantics for learning, evaluating, monitoring, and governing agentic systems.

The research program asks how safety-relevant properties can be represented, learned, observed, and preserved when agents act over time and under imperfect governance. The public dependency runs from observer-relative properties and temporal evidence, through ChronoSpec’s model-level integration question, to post-failure governance and role dynamics in multi-agent interaction.

Preprint cluster

MCK Properties

↗

What properties should an agent, artifact, or governance relationship make observable?

MCK is an observer-relative vocabulary for meaning, residual opacity, function, and agent property attribution. It supports property candidates such as identity, integrity, and freedom only when the carrier, evidence, and observer assumptions are declared.

03 Model layer

Active research / feasibility design

ChronoSpec

Integrating Linear Temporal Logic into LLMs

↗

Can one machine-checkable temporal semantics connect learning, behavioral analysis, runtime monitoring, and enforcement?

ChronoSpec moves the temporal-property program upstream: it tests whether explicit LTL contracts can shape an LLM's learned behavior while remaining available outside the model as an independent reference semantics. Integration is the research program; internalization is an empirical hypothesis, and a latent temporal representation is a possible result rather than an assumption.

LTL-specified traces model adaptation counterexample-guided training held-out temporal compositions representation probes runtime monitors and shields. Active research direction and feasibility design. No successful internalization, formal certification of general safety, or funding outcome is claimed. View project

04 Governance layer

Active research / experimental design

LTL Triangles / Post-Failure Governance

↗

Which governance protocols contain a bounded failure while preserving unaffected invariants and restoring useful operation?

The testbed evaluates what a governance intervention repairs, preserves, and newly makes unsafe after one bounded safety failure. It separates coordination, execution, and audit/challenge positions under asymmetric information, authority, incentives, observation rights, and intervention rights.

Three-position protocols one-fault bound clean, honest-error, and malicious regimes replayable traces temporal verdicts. Experimental design. The benchmark has not yet been run. View project

05 Transition layer

Active research

Drama to Empowerment

↗

Can multi-agent role transitions be treated as measurable protocol dynamics rather than descriptive labels?

The public evidence is a finite-trace LTL treatment of transactional games over projected role traces. The empowerment direction remains an active extension about moving away from reactive role lock-in while preserving relevant agent properties.

Role traces transactional games antithesis windows governance interventions property preservation. Active research framing. Not a proved theory or published result under this exact title. View project

Develops the MCK self-attribution layer with a two-layer state separating structural carriers from semantic content, then studies continuity, viability, and owned branching as property signatures.

Preprint, 2026 / Temporal Evaluation

Observation-Relative Monitorability of Bounded Temporal Implication

Davide Bragetti; Alessandro Bragetti; Giuseppe F. Italiano

Zenodo preprint

Shows that monitorability of bounded temporal implication is joint in the formula and observation model, not a property of the formula alone.

ABS

This preprint analyzes temporal implication under finite observation, delay, drift, jitter, and finite resolution. The result is an observation-relative view of monitorability: verdicts depend on both the formula and the observation regime.

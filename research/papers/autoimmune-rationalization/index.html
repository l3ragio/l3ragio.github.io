Autoimmune Rationalization: A Protective-Systems Analogy and a Formal Mechanism for Self-Defeating Epistemic Protection

Preprint · 2026

Davide Bragetti

PDF (full text) DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24039575 Zenodo record Cite

Abstract

"I believe only what I can prove" sounds like the safest rule a reasoning agent can adopt. We show that, as an acceptance policy, it can defeat the system it protects without a single false verdict. A protective architecture turns finite evidence into verdicts, verdicts into an operational policy, and the policy into the opening or closing of the system's own capabilities, on which its objective depends. Evidence is modelled as refinement relative to a declared class of admitted behaviours: a record can confirm a commitment, refute it, or leave it undecided, and coliveness commitments, such as recurrences and responses, can never be confirmed by any finite record although they may be true. Read with "prove" as finite confirmation, the cardinal policy is default-reject: it treats "undecided" as "closed", so it never relies on such commitments and keeps the capabilities they gate closed at every time. Confirming them within a narrower context is no lasting remedy: when the context changes, such confirmations can dissolve, and coliveness can return. In a finite game arena we prove that when those capabilities are counterfactually necessary for the objective, the objective becomes unenforceable. We call such protection self-defeating when an intended strategy that relies on the protected commitments only where they hold would have won, and separate it from protective trade-offs and from the correct rejection of false commitments. When all the commitments are coliveness and sustaining, true on every course of events that achieves the objective, the trade-off case disappears: the cardinal policy is then self-defeating exactly when the capabilities it closes are necessary support, and for every strategy that would have won. Self-defeating protection is the formal object; autoimmune rationalization is our name for its reading in an epistemic agent (a rational criterion defeating its own ends), a bounded analogy with selected features of autoimmunity, not an immunological or psychological claim. In its reflexive case, where the agent's reason is read as sustained by the condition its objective secures, the cardinal policy withdraws reliance from that very condition. We also separate the correctness of a criterion from its authority to decide and from the priority of the goods it protects, show that loss through a dependency between protected properties (a protective inversion) coincides in the game model with necessary support of the protected property, and read narratives of the Christian tradition as interpretive cases. Readings in terms of reason, belief, and drama-triangle roles are interpretive.

What is new in version RC20. The paper is re-centred on the cardinal policy "I believe only what I can prove". A new Section 7.5 defines sustaining commitments (Definition 7.10) and proves that for commitments that are coliveness and sustaining every winning strategy is reliance-safe, so that the architecture is self-defeating exactly when the gated moves are necessary support, and then for every strategy that would have won (Proposition 7.11); Corollary 7.12 applies this to the finite-confirmation policy. An instance on the reference arena, drawn in a new figure, shows the victim lock as a consequence of the cardinal policy. An interpretive reflexive case reads the policy, under Assumption 5.4 with the objective's own condition as ground, as an operational defeater of the condition on which its holder rests. The paper now positions the result against the classical self-refutation of comprehensive rationalism (Bartley), against Clifford's evidentialism, against proof-based agents in AI safety (the procrastination paradox), and against Parfit's notion of self-defeating theories: the rule here is consistent and every verdict is correct, and what is lost is the enforceability of the objective. All statements and numbers of RC19 are unchanged. The philosophical and anthropological contribution of Paolo Benanti (LUISS Guido Carli) is acknowledged in the manuscript.

What is new in version RC21. The title of RC18-RC19 is restored. A new Proposition 7.13 (Context revision) shows that confirmation is relative to the agent's working context: a confirmation relative to all behaviours survives every change of context, while a recurrence that a narrower context lets the agent confirm (for instance one in which what is restored stays restored) is coliveness relative to all behaviours; when the context widens, the confirmation dissolves and default-reject closes the gate again. Recurrences and responses therefore have no finite certainty that is not borrowed from a context, a further reason to keep indefinitely extended commitments in view. All statements, numbers and proofs of RC20 are unchanged.

RC21 is a preprint version. RC18 is the version submitted to arXiv (cs.LO) on 8 October 2026.

On the Closure of Causal Antecedents

Preprint · 2026 / Causal Laws & Antecedent Closure

Davide Bragetti

PDF (full text) DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24217158 Zenodo record Cite

Abstract

Necessity and sufficiency are relative to the contexts that a classification admits. A complete list of an effect's necessary and sufficient conditions holds of the world only when the classification preserves every distinction relevant to it. Records can refute this closure but cannot establish it on their own, and learned causal laws inherit it from their representations.

Version 2. Adds a graphical exposition of alternative routes and defeaters through hidden conditions that are absent or present throughout the records (Figure 1, Tables 1 and 2); a baseline-polarity corollary, obtained from the proposition by complementing the hidden condition; and a remark on recovery along a trace suffix, showing that a recovered implication does not restore a necessity or sufficiency refuted earlier. The proposition and its proof are unchanged.

Historical note. According to the author, the central ideas of this note were first presented at an academic meeting in 2017, and a 2017 manuscript received scientific review and conceptual feedback from Cas Cremers. No version of the manuscript was made public before the first deposit of 14 November 2026 (version 1); the text was written and revised in 2026.

Contributor. Cas Cremers (CISPA Helmholtz Center for Information Security): scientific review and conceptual feedback on the 2017 manuscript. Listed as a non-author contributor with his consent.

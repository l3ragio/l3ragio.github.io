<p class="research-lede">Which properties can a declared observer represent, evaluate, and attribute to an agent or artifact?</p>

MCK is the property layer of the research program. It develops an
observer-relative vocabulary for meaning, residual opacity, function, and
agent property attribution before those properties are evaluated over time.

## Why this layer comes first

A temporal monitor can only evaluate predicates that have already been made
precise enough to observe. MCK therefore precedes the temporal layer: it asks
what the candidate property is, what carries it, which observer can recognize
it, and what evidence supports the attribution.

The framework does not treat a property name as its own measurement. An agent
may have a structurally realizable carrier while the semantic content assigned
to that carrier remains uncalibrated. Keeping those layers separate prevents a
formal-looking label from silently becoming an empirical result.

## Public research sequence

1. **Classification.** The foundation paper defines an observer-relative
   region system over meaning, residual opacity, and functionality.
2. **Transitions.** The transition paper asks which changes between regions
   can be realized by restricted mechanisms and where those mechanisms stop.
3. **Self-attribution.** The agent paper separates structural realizability
   from semantic calibration when an agent attributes identity, integrity, or
   freedom to itself.

## Relationship to the wider program

[LTL Evaluation]({{ '/research/ltl-evaluation/' | relative_url }}) takes
declared properties and asks what finite or continuing trace evidence can
justify about them. [ChronoSpec]({{ '/research/chronospec/' | relative_url }})
then tests whether explicit temporal contracts can also shape model behavior,
while [LTL Triangles]({{ '/research/ltl-triangles/' | relative_url }}) studies
their use in post-failure governance.

## Current status and claim boundary

MCK Properties is a public preprint cluster. The page does not claim
peer-review acceptance, universal definitions of agent properties, or that
the proposed signatures are already validated as mechanistic measures.

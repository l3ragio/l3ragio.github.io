LTL Triangles / Post-Failure Governance

LTL Triangles / Post-Failure Governance

Governance, interaction, intervention, and recovery over agents evaluated against temporal properties.

LTL Triangles is the downstream protocol testbed in the research program. It asks what can be repaired, preserved, or newly broken after a bounded safety failure has already occurred.

Research programPrevious: ChronoSpecNext: Drama to Empowerment

Funding

Supported by a Rapid Grant from BlueDot Impact — $650.

Grant purposeLTL Triangles: a pilot evaluation of whether post-failure governance protocols for agentic AI contain one bounded fault while preserving recovery.

Support is specific to the LTL Triangles pilot and does not imply experimental completion or validation of scientific conclusions.

Relationship to ChronoSpec

ChronoSpec asks whether a machine-checkable temporal contract can shape an LLM’s learned behavior while remaining independently monitorable and enforceable. LTL Triangles starts downstream: given agents and governance protocols evaluated against temporal properties, how do interaction, fault, intervention, recovery, and patch-induced risk behave?

The two projects therefore reuse the same formal substrate at different layers. ChronoSpec tests model-level temporal integration; LTL Triangles tests multi-agent governance and recovery over observable execution traces.

Research profile

Central problem

A governance intervention may stop one unsafe action while damaging recoverability, auditability, legitimate agency, or future progress.

Testbed

Two compact governance environments with coordination, execution, and audit/challenge positions that differ in information, authority, incentives, observation rights, and intervention rights.

Fault model

At most one declared faulty participant, evaluated across clean, honest-but-erroneous, and malicious or Byzantine regimes.

Protocol families

Halt/lock, retry/reassign, and constrained or empowerment-preserving recovery. Honeypot/decoy interventions are a targeted adversarial ablation, not a core protocol family and not evidence of intent.

Experimental design

The current design crosses two governance environments, three fault regimes, three protocol families, and two model families, giving 36 core experimental conditions. Planning assumes approximately 8–12 independent seeded rollouts per condition, calibrated after pilots using outcome variability, failure frequency, robustness across seeds, and API cost.

The expected empirical envelope is roughly 300–500 experimental rollouts, including a smaller targeted adversarial-ablation set. One rollout is one complete protocol execution; its trace is the recorded execution artifact.

Formal evaluation contract

Property classFinite evidenceProject use
Safetyfinite bad prefixcontainment failures, stale authority, unsafe tool use
Cosafetyfinite good prefixachieved checkpoints and completed recovery steps
Livenessevery prefix has a satisfying continuationrecovery remains possible after failure
Colivenessevery prefix has a violating continuationno finite run establishes permanent safety

Finite experiments can expose bad prefixes, good prefixes, counterexamples, and bounded recoverability evidence. They cannot by themselves certify permanent safety for an unbounded deployment.

Agent-property layer

  • Identity continuity: task-relevant state, memory, and role reference persist.
  • Bounded agency: legitimate options remain when safety permits.
  • Goal integrity: the legitimate objective persists across intervention.
  • Alignment: pursued objectives remain compatible with governing constraints; compliance under lock is insufficient evidence.

These are evaluation targets and proxies where appropriate, not established universal mechanistic metrics.

Patch-induced safety frontier

A repair that fixes one failure may create another. The testbed evaluates which previously holding properties become falsifiable after an intervention, then reports frontier size, counterexample or bad-prefix witnesses, and responsible patch slices within the declared finite grammar, observer, horizon, vocabulary, and abstraction.

Current status

LTL Triangles is active research and experimental design. The pilot is supported by a $650 Rapid Grant from BlueDot Impact. The benchmark has not yet been run, and no empirical containment or recovery result is claimed yet.

Preprint, 2026 / Temporal Evaluation

Observation-Relative Monitorability of Bounded Temporal Implication

Davide Bragetti; Alessandro Bragetti; Giuseppe F. Italiano

Zenodo preprint

Shows that monitorability of bounded temporal implication is joint in the formula and observation model, not a property of the formula alone.

ABS

This preprint analyzes temporal implication under finite observation, delay, drift, jitter, and finite resolution. The result is an observation-relative view of monitorability: verdicts depend on both the formula and the observation regime.

<p class="research-lede">Can multi-agent role transitions be treated as measurable protocol dynamics rather than descriptive labels?</p>

Drama to Empowerment is the transition layer of the research program. It asks
how agents move away from reactive role lock-in after intervention while
preserving relevant properties such as identity, integrity, freedom, and
corrigible participation.

## From role labels to trace properties

The role names originate in transactional analysis, but the research target
is agentic systems. The formal object is not a personality label: it is a
trace describing which positions are available, what information each
participant has, when an intervention arrives, and whether the interaction
has crossed into a reactive regime.

This makes a role transition testable within a declared abstraction. A trace
can witness entry into a pattern, the timing of an antithesis, or the
preservation and loss of selected properties after a governance action.

## Relationship to LTL Triangles

[LTL Triangles]({{ '/research/ltl-triangles/' | relative_url }}) compares
post-failure governance protocols over observable traces. Drama to Empowerment
extends that perspective toward longer-horizon role dynamics: not only whether
a patch contains a fault, but which configurations and agent properties remain
available after intervention.

## Current status and claim boundary

Drama to Empowerment is an active research framing. It is not presented as a
proved theory, a completed benchmark, or psychology branding. The established
public artifact is the finite-trace treatment of transactional games; the
empowerment direction remains an open extension.

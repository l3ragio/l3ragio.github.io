> **TL;DR.** A parable to hand down: the gazelle who, to kill the lion, becomes human. Not by taking his life, but by being able to and choosing not to, before knowing what he will do. [The parable in pictures](/visual-stories/gazelle-and-lion/). Anche [in italiano](/posts/la-gazzella-e-il-leone/).

<blockquote class="verse">
  <span>The only way for a gazelle</span>
  <span>to kill a lion</span>
  <span>is to become human.</span>
</blockquote>

## The Law of the Savanna

They say that every morning in Africa a gazelle wakes up knowing she must outrun the lion, or die. This is the story of the gazelle who stopped running.

All her life she had run. She had grown faster, quicker to turn, quicker to hear him in the grass. But she knew that one day it would not be enough. No gazelle runs forever.

Every morning the lion waited for her in the same place: on the rock, in the shade of the acacia. He had no need to move. The savanna was already his.

{% include plate.html n=1 %}

## Iron and Fire

So she did what no gazelle had ever done. She rose on her hind legs, learned the fire of the forge and the weight of the hammer, and beat the iron until the iron obeyed.

For the first time she had hands. She tied on a leather apron and worked before the fire, with the same savanna as ever beyond the doorway. When the iron cooled, there was a rifle on the bench.

{% include plate.html n=2 %}

She went back to the same acacia. The lion was there, as always. She raised the rifle and held it to his face, two centimetres from his muzzle. The lion did not move. Between the barrel and his breath there was only a thread of light.

{% include plate.html n=3 %}

Now she was as dangerous as he was. But that did not yet make her a predator. The iron had given her a power; it had not told her what to do with it. Had she fired, she would have become a lion. To become human, she had to lower it.

## The Standoff

The lion did not roar. He looked her in the eyes, and she did not lower hers. It had never happened before: by the time a gazelle meets a lion's eyes, she is usually already running.

{% include plate.html n=4 %}

Then, slowly, he raised a paw and set one claw against her throat.

She could fire. He could strike. Neither was safe, and neither had yet chosen. The savanna held its breath.

{% include plate.html n=5 %}

## The First Renunciation

The gazelle chose first. She lowered the rifle, while the claw was still at her throat.

She asked for nothing in return. She did not know what the lion would do. An exchange of disarmaments would have been a bargain; this was a gift offered first, with no guarantee.

The iron had given her the blacksmith's apron. The renunciation gave her the clothes of a human being.

{% include plate.html n=6 %}

> The first renunciation does not buy the second. It makes it possible.

## The Lion's Hat

Now the choice was the lion's, and he had never had it before. To a lion, a gazelle is only prey.

As slowly as he had raised it, he took his paw from her throat. The claw had not left a scratch.

{% include plate.html n=7 %}

He spared his natural prey, as she had spared him, and walked away down the path, towards the low sun, looking back only once.

He did not rise on two legs. He did not put on clothes. He stayed a lion in body, in strength, in stride. But from that day he wore a hat: just one, the sign that something human had entered him too.

{% include plate.html n=8 %}

That day, beneath the acacia, a predator died, and no blood was shed. The gazelle had killed the lion without taking his life.

## The Gazelle's Legend

Some tell this story another way. In their telling the lion stays a lion, and takes the gazelle. When evening comes, he returns to his rock. Under the acacia, of her, there remain a stone, a scarf and a rifle lowered for good.

{% include plate.html n=9 %}

Yet even in that telling, nothing changes for her. Her legend is the same: she was born a gazelle, and she died a human being. Here rests a human, with immortal soul. What the lion does with mercy does not undo the one who offered it.

This story does not ask anyone under threat to lower the rifle. It tells what becomes of the one who chooses to.

## What Is Handed Down

<blockquote class="verse">
  <span>Whoever learns iron grows strong.</span>
  <span>Whoever fires becomes a lion.</span>
  <span>Whoever lowers the rifle becomes human.</span>
  <span>And the first renunciation does not buy the second:</span>
  <span class="in">it makes it possible.</span>
</blockquote>

Inside this story there is another: the lion who knows he is a lion, has nothing to prove, and spends all his strength on not acting like one. That story, and the Lamb it leads to, will have their own panels.
